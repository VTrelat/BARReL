\section{Case Study: Comparing the Tools}
\label{sec:proof-workflow}
\label{sec:case-study}

We compare the Atelier~B and \barrel proof workflows on the six-component B development of the IEEE~1394 tree identify protocol by Abrial, Cansell, and M\'ery~\cite{AbrialCansellMery2003}, which refines a leader-election algorithm.\footnote{We thank Dominique M\'ery for giving us access to the B refinement chain and for the insightful discussions that helped us understand it.}
The artifacts contain the B sources in \texttt{specs/leader/} and both sets of proof scripts in \texttt{examples/leader/}, together with the counterexamples from \cref{sec:proof-safety}.
We ran Atelier~B's Force~3 followed by its predicate prover (\texttt{pp}) on the generated obligations, then completed the remaining proofs interactively.

This case study exercises the requirements identified in \cref{sec:proof-safety}: partial expressions carry explicit WD proofs, and the framework can be extended with reusable (kernel-checked) lemmas.
We examine how \barrel supports invariant-preservation proofs after updates, reuse across refinements, and extensions to automation while maintaining these checks.

\subsection{Reusable mathematical lemmas}
\label{sec:workflow-lemmas}

The rule instance examined in \cref{sec:checked-rules} motivates requiring proofs of the general facts used to discharge obligations.
Users can extend \barrel with lemmas proved using Mathlib, through the same mechanism as the built-in library described in \cref{par:automation}.
The function updates in the leader-election development illustrate how one such lemma serves both invariant preservation and definedness proofs.

\begin{example}[Preserving totality after a function update]
\label{ex:workflow-update}
The refinement \texttt{mm3} records the confirmations received at each node in a function $\mathtt{sn} \in \mathtt{ND} \to \mathcal{P}(\mathtt{ND})$.
Its operation \texttt{receive\_cnf} records a confirmation from node $\mathtt{xx}$ at node $\mathtt{yy}$ by updating $\mathtt{sn}(\mathtt{yy})$ to $\mathtt{sn}(\mathtt{yy}) \cup \{\mathtt{xx}\}$.
Preserving the total-function invariant is an instance of the general property
\begin{equation}
  {f \in A \to B} \;\land\; {x \in A} \;\land\; {y \in B}
  \quad\implies\quad
  f \bovr \{x \mapsto y\} \,\in\, A \to B.
  \label{eq:workflow-override}
\end{equation}
Here $f \bovr \{x \mapsto y\}$ denotes the relation obtained by replacing the value of $f$ at $x$ with $y$.
The lemma \texttt{tfun\_\allowbreak override\_\allowbreak singleton} proves \cref{eq:workflow-override} for arbitrary carrier types using Mathlib's sets and relations:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean, basicstyle=\small\ttfamily, numbers=none]
@[tfun] theorem tfun_override_singleton {α$$ β$$ : Type*}
  {A : Set α$$} {B : Set β$$} {f : SetRel α$$ β$$} {x : α$$} {y : β$$}
  (hf : f ∈$$ A ⟶$$ B) (hx : x ∈$$ A) (hy : y ∈$$ B) :
    f <+ {(x, y)} ∈$$ A ⟶$$ B :=$$ ⋯
\end{lstlisting}
The proof in \texttt{MM3.lean} applies this lemma with $A := \mathtt{ND}$, $B := \mathcal{P}(\mathtt{ND})$, $x := \mathtt{yy}$, and $y := \mathtt{sn}(\mathtt{yy}) \cup \{\mathtt{xx}\}$.
The operation's guard and the invariant provide the node memberships; the remaining premise is that the updated set is a subset of $\mathtt{ND}$.
\end{example}

The same lemma is applied in \texttt{MM4.lean} to the function \texttt{ba}, which records acknowledgments at each node.
Our Atelier~B scripts establish the corresponding totality claims separately in the contexts of these two obligations.
Atelier~B's related rule \texttt{InFunctionXY.22} establishes preservation of partial-function typing under overriding; totality also requires a domain argument.
Atelier~B supports assertions and user rules, but applying a rule does not require a kernel-checked proof of its correctness.
In Lean, each use of the general lemma supplies its premises and is checked by the kernel.

The totality result also provides the first premise of \texttt{app.WD\_of\_mem\_tfun}.
Together with a proof that the argument belongs to the domain, it establishes the definedness of application to the updated function (\cref{lst:min-fn-app}).
The \texttt{@[tfun]} annotation registers the lemma for total-function reasoning, so the WD tactic can perform this composition automatically using the mechanism described in \cref{par:automation}.

\subsection{Refinement with explicit proof dependencies}
\label{sec:refinement-dependencies}

The reuse in \cref{ex:workflow-update} concerns a general mathematical lemma.
Refinement also creates dependencies between proofs of individual components: the WD parameters of encoded operators must be supplied in expressions inherited from an abstract component.
This is the cross-import reuse introduced in \cref{sec:innner-workings}.
When that component has already been proved in Lean, \barrel can supply the required proof arguments by applying its WD theorems.
Lean checks these applications in the refinement's context, so reuse avoids a new proof obligation without omitting the definedness check.



After \texttt{qed}, a component's WD theorems are available to subsequent imports, including across Lean modules.
Where possible, \barrel removes unnecessary premises from their reusable statements, allowing later components to apply them with fewer hypotheses.
If no available theorem applies, \barrel generates a new WD obligation.

\begin{example}[Reusing definedness across refinements]
\label{ex:workflow-refinement}
In \texttt{mm1}, an assertion states that when all neighbors of a node $x$ are its children in the current tree $\mathtt{tr}$, the tree assigned to $x$ by $\mathtt{ff}$ is $\mathtt{tr}$.
The refinement \texttt{mm2} inherits this assertion, including the application $\mathtt{ff}(x)$.
Definedness of this application needs only
\[
  \mathtt{ff} \in \mathtt{ND} \to (\mathtt{ND} \pfun \mathtt{ND})
  \quad\land\quad x \in \mathtt{ND}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathtt{app.WD}\ \mathtt{ff}\ x.
\]
Atelier~B generates WD obligations for the assertion in \texttt{mm1} and does not generate them again in \texttt{mm2}.
In \barrel, importing \texttt{MM1.lean} into \texttt{MM2.lean} makes the proved WD fact available when encoding the refinement.
All 53 occurrences of the inherited application are then justified by applications of \texttt{mm1}'s theorem, with no new WD obligation in \texttt{mm2}.
Lean checks each theorem application using the hypotheses available in \texttt{mm2}.
This reuse justifies the inherited expression; the assertion itself remains a separate proof obligation.

\end{example}

Across the six components, Atelier~B generates 60 WD obligations from the B specification.
\barrel instead extracts WD conditions while translating individual expressions and shares their proofs between occurrences.
It generates 31 WD goals, all proved automatically, and avoids 7 further goals by reusing three previously proved theorems across components.
These goal counts exclude repeated theorem applications, such as the 53 in \texttt{mm2}; every encoded partial application still carries a WD proof.

\subsection{Automation, interaction, and trust}

Atelier~B's automation, developed specifically for B over more than twenty years, leaves fewer obligations for interactive proof in this development.
The examples in \cref{sec:proof-safety} motivate considering both automation coverage and how individual proof steps are checked.
In \barrel, Lean's kernel checks the resulting proof terms and the proofs of every lemma they use.

Interactive proof can also help extend trustworthy automation in \barrel.
In \cref{ex:workflow-update}, the proved totality lemma supplies a reusable step for the WD tactic; \cref{ex:workflow-refinement} shows how previously proved WD theorems can be reused across imports.
With these generic lemmas and extended premise search, \barrel automatically discharges all locally generated WD goals in the leader-election development.
Adding a tactic or lemma does not add a trusted inference rule: its use must produce a proof term accepted by Lean.

The proof environment also affects the effort needed when automation fails.
Atelier~B displays goals, hypotheses, and proof histories; the message ``The Predicate Prover failed to prove the current goal'' does not distinguish missing premises from unsuccessful search.
Lean lets the user isolate a claim with \texttt{have}; applying a theorem exposes its remaining premises as subgoals, and a failed \texttt{exact} reports the supplied and expected types.
These diagnostics provide a concrete point at which to investigate the failed step, although matching and normalization can still require manual work.

Using \barrel requires B users to learn Lean and the relevant Mathlib abstractions, which may be a barrier to adoption. In return, Lean checks the proofs produced by automation and the lemmas they use, including WD proofs reused across components. The comparison therefore considers both the interactive effort required and the checks supporting the resulting proofs.
